About this book

Measuring what you cannot see

Ability, attitude and anxiety cannot be observed directly. Item response theory (IRT) measures them from the answers people give to individual items, and it sits behind international assessments, adaptive tests and modern questionnaires. This book is written for postgraduate students, researchers and lecturers, including readers from non mathematical backgrounds.

By the end, you will be able to

  1. Explain why hidden traits are hard to measure, what IRT is, and where it is used.Chapter 1
  2. State the assumptions of IRT, explain each model’s formula, and describe how the models developed.Chapter 2
  3. Explain the likelihood function and how θ, difficulty and discrimination are estimated from data.Chapter 3
  4. Compare the main estimation algorithms and what each assumes.Chapter 4
  5. Judge whether estimates can be trusted, using precision and fit.Chapter 5

How to use this book

Every scene follows five steps

Step 1ListenDr Priya explains the idea, line by line, with her voice and words together.
Step 2WatchThe picture moves with her words: points slide, curves draw, bars grow.
Step 3ReadA short reading explains the idea fully, followed by the key idea and the formal idea.
Step 4Try“Your turn” controls let you test the idea, with a live line that reads your screen.
Step 5Move onPress Next when you are ready. The story waits while you read.

Key words

WordMeaning
ItemOne question, task or statement.
Trait, θThe hidden quality being measured, such as maths ability.
Difficulty, bWhere an item sits on the scale: the level at which a person has a 50:50 chance.
LogitThe unit of the IRT scale, like a centimetre on a ruler.
ParameterA hidden number the model needs, such as θ or b.
EstimateOur best value for a parameter, worked out from the answers.
